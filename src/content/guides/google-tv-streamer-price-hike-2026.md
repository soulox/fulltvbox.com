## The short version

- **What happened:** Google quietly raised the [Google TV Streamer](/reviews/google-tv-streamer-4k-2024)'s price from $99.99 to $149.99 on August 31, 2026 — a 50% jump — citing rising component costs. The hardware is unchanged.
- **Why it matters:** at $99.99 the Streamer was an easy recommendation. At $149.99 it's competing directly with pricier, more capable boxes instead of budget streamers.
- **Who should still buy it:** anyone who specifically wants its Matter/Thread smart-home hub and Gemini AI search baked into the box — that combination still doesn't exist anywhere else at any price.
- **Who should look elsewhere:** anyone buying it just for solid 4K/Dolby Vision streaming — the [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-max-2024) does that for a third of the price, and the [Apple TV 4K](/reviews/apple-tv-4k-3rd-gen) now undercuts it while offering more.

Streaming-device prices almost never move without a hardware refresh attached. This one did — and it changes the shopping math for anyone who had "get a Google TV Streamer" on their list.

## What actually happened

On August 31, 2026, Google raised the price of the Google TV Streamer (4K) from $99.99 to $149.99 in the US — a straight 50% increase, in both the Porcelain and Hazel colorways. Reporting on the change (from outlets including 9to5Google, Android Central, and TechRadar) says Google attributed it to rising component costs, without naming a specific part. The timing lines up with broader reporting on memory and component price pressure across consumer electronics this year.

What didn't change: the box itself. It's still the same 2024 hardware — MediaTek MT8696 chip, 4GB of RAM, 32GB of storage, Wi-Fi 6, Ethernet, Dolby Vision, and the built-in Matter/Thread smart-home radio. Nothing was added, and nothing was fixed. You're paying $50 more for an identical product.

That's worth being direct about: this isn't a "new and improved" story. It's a straightforward price increase on a two-year-old device, and it's the kind of change that's easy to miss if you're going off an old bookmark or a review written before September.

## Does it still make sense at $149.99?

At $99.99, the Google TV Streamer's pitch was simple: it undercut the [Apple TV 4K](/reviews/apple-tv-4k-3rd-gen) and [NVIDIA Shield TV Pro](/reviews/nvidia-shield-tv-pro-2025) while still offering Ethernet, decent RAM, and a smart-home hub neither competitor has. At $149.99, that math flips.

- **Apple TV 4K** — the site's review lists it around $129, so the Streamer now costs *more* than Apple's box, which has a faster chip and tighter integration with iPhones and HomeKit. If you're in the Apple ecosystem at all, there's no longer a price reason to pick Google's device instead.
- **NVIDIA Shield TV Pro** — around $199, it's still pricier than the Streamer, but the gap has shrunk from $100 to $50. The Shield's AI upscaling and stronger gaming/Plex performance make that a much easier $50 to justify than it used to be. See our [Google TV Streamer review](/reviews/google-tv-streamer-4k-2024) and [NVIDIA Shield TV Pro review](/reviews/nvidia-shield-tv-pro-2025) for the full spec-by-spec comparison.
- **Fire TV Stick 4K Max** — around $59, it delivers the same practical result for most people (4K, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos, snappy performance) at roughly a third of the new price. See the [full review](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-max-2024).

The one thing none of those alternatives replicate is the Streamer's built-in Matter/Thread smart-home hub paired with Gemini AI search. If that combination is specifically why you wanted this device, it's still the only box that does it — you're just paying more for the privilege now. ## What's NOT worth it right now

- **Paying $149.99 just for "the Google TV interface."** You can get essentially the same Google TV software experience on the [Chromecast with Google TV (4K)](/reviews/chromecast-google-tv-4k) for about $49. You lose Ethernet, the extra RAM, and the Matter hub, but if none of those matter to you, the Chromecast is the far better value now.
- **Treating this as a "new model."** It isn't. If you already own a Google TV Streamer, there's nothing here that makes yours worse or obsolete — this is a pricing change for new buyers, not a product update.
- **Buying on brand loyalty alone.** With the price gap to Apple TV 4K and NVIDIA Shield TV Pro this small, "I already use Google stuff" isn't a strong enough reason on its own anymore — compare the actual specs in each device's review before deciding.

## What to buy instead, by priority

- **Want the cheapest solid 4K box:** [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-max-2024) — 4K, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos, for a fraction of the Streamer's new price.
- **Want the Google TV interface specifically, on a budget:** [Chromecast with Google TV (4K)](/reviews/chromecast-google-tv-4k) — same Google TV software, no Ethernet or hub, roughly a third of the price.
- **Want the strongest all-round performer and don't mind spending more:** [NVIDIA Shield TV Pro](/reviews/nvidia-shield-tv-pro-2025) — now only $50 above the Streamer, with better gaming, Plex transcoding, and AI upscaling.
- **Deep in the Apple ecosystem:** [Apple TV 4K (3rd Gen)](/reviews/apple-tv-4k-3rd-gen) — now cheaper than the Streamer, with tighter AirPlay/HomeKit integration.
- **Specifically need the Matter/Thread hub plus Gemini search in one box:** the [Google TV Streamer](/reviews/google-tv-streamer-4k-2024) is still the only device that does both — it's just no longer the budget pick it used to be.
