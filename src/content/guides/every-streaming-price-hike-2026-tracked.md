## The short version

- **2026 has been a non-stop wave of streaming price increases** — at least seven major services raised prices this year, roughly one every month or two.
- **Paramount+ (Jan), Netflix (Mar), Prime Video's ad-free add-on (Apr), Fubo (Jun), Apple TV+ and Peacock (Aug), and ESPN, Disney+, and Hulu (Sep)** have all confirmed increases.
- **Ad-free tiers took the biggest hits** — typically $2–2.50/month per service — while ad-supported tiers crept up by $0.50–1.
- **The takeaway:** a four-service household is paying noticeably more for the exact same lineup than it was in January, even without adding anything new.

We update this page as new increases are confirmed. Prices below are standard U.S. list prices as of early October 2026 — always check a service's own signup page before you buy, since promos and regional pricing vary.

## The 2026 timeline, month by month

**January 15 — Paramount+.** The ad-supported Essential tier rose a dollar to $8.99/month ($89.99/year), and the ad-free Premium tier (which now includes Showtime) rose a dollar to $13.99/month ($139.99/year).

**March 26 — Netflix.** Every U.S. plan went up at once: the ad-supported tier moved from $7.99 to $8.99, Standard from $17.99 to $19.99, and Premium from $24.99 to $26.99. Extra-member add-on pricing rose too.

**April 10 — Prime Video.** Amazon didn't touch the base Prime membership price, but the fee to remove ads from Prime Video jumped from $2.99 to $4.99/month — a 67% increase on that one line item.

**June — Fubo.** After temporarily cutting prices during a carriage dispute with NBCUniversal in late 2025, Fubo raised its Pro plan again once NBCUniversal's channels were restored, landing close to $89/month.

**August 18 — Peacock.** Select (ads) rose from $7.99 to $8.99, Premium (ads) from $10.99 to $12.99, and Premium Plus (ad-free) from $16.99 to $19.99/month. The Apple TV+/Peacock bundles rose by similar amounts.

**August — Apple TV+.** The monthly price jumped $2 to $14.99, and the annual plan rose from $99.99 to $119.99 — Apple's fourth increase in four years.

**September 17 — ESPN.** ESPN Unlimited rose from $29.99 to $31.99/month ($299.99 to $319.99/year), and ESPN Select rose from $12.99 to $13.99/month ($129.99 to $139.99/year).

**September 23 — Disney+ and Hulu.** Both standalone ad-free plans rose $2.50 to $21.49/month (Disney+'s annual plan went from $189.99 to $214.99), the ad-free Disney+/Hulu bundle rose $2 to $21.99/month, and Hulu's ad-supported tier rose 50 cents to $12.49/month.

Several live-TV services, including Sling TV, have also quietly raised prices on specific plans this year without the same wide press coverage — if you're on a live-TV plan, check your most recent bill against the price you signed up for. Fubo's increase is a special case worth explaining: it briefly cut prices in late 2025 during a carriage dispute that pulled NBCUniversal channels (including NBC, Bravo, and USA) off the service, then raised prices again once that dispute was resolved and the channels came back — so some subscribers saw a price *drop* before this year's increase, not just a hike.

Not every major service touched its price this year. Max (formerly HBO Max) last raised prices in late 2025, and its listed plans have held steady through 2026 so far — a reminder that "every service raises prices every year" isn't quite true, even if it can feel that way.

## What's actually going on

None of these increases happened in a vacuum. A few things are driving all of them at once:

- **Content and sports-rights costs keep climbing.** ESPN and Fubo's increases are both tied directly to the rising cost of live-sports rights, which only gets more expensive as more leagues and games move behind streaming paywalls.
- **Wall Street wants streaming to be profitable, not just big.** Parent companies that spent years subsidizing subscriber growth with cheap introductory pricing are now expected to show real margins on their streaming divisions — a price increase is the fastest lever to pull, and it's an easier sell to investors than cutting content spend.
- **This isn't most services' first hike.** Apple TV+, Disney+, and Peacock have each raised prices multiple times in just the last few years, and the gap between what a service charged at launch and what it charges now keeps widening. Expect the pattern to continue into 2027 rather than reverse — nothing about the underlying cost pressures is going away.
- **Ad-supported tiers are the pressure valve.** Notice that almost every increase above hit the ad-free tier harder than the ad-supported one. Services are quietly using the ad tier as the "stay cheap" option while pushing people who want an ad-free experience to pay a growing premium for it.

## What's NOT worth doing in response

- **Don't cancel everything in a panic.** A $2/month increase on one service rarely changes the math on whether streaming beats cable — see [is cutting the cord still worth it in 2026](/guides/is-cutting-the-cord-worth-it-2026) for the bigger picture.
- **Don't add a bundle just because it's "on sale."** Bundling ESPN into your Disney+/Hulu plan to soften the sting of its own price increase only helps if you'd actually watch ESPN — our [streaming bundles guide](/guides/streaming-bundles-worth-it-2026) breaks down which bundles are genuine savings and which are a trap.
- **Don't assume every service raised prices this year.** Several haven't — don't preemptively downgrade or cancel a service that's still at the price you signed up for.

## How to actually keep your bill flat

1. **Move to ad-supported tiers where you can tolerate it.** Every service above still has a meaningfully cheaper ad tier, and the gap to ad-free has only grown this year.
2. **Audit your subscriptions quarterly.** It's easy to forget you're still paying for a service you binged once in the spring. Cancel anything you haven't opened in a month.
3. **Rotate instead of stacking.** There's no contract on any of these services — subscribe, binge, cancel, move to the next one.
4. **Re-run your numbers after every hike.** Our free [streaming cost calculator](/cost-calculator) lets you total your exact stack at current prices in a few seconds, so you know immediately when a hike actually changes your math.
