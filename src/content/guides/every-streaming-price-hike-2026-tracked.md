## The short version

- **At least seven major services raised US prices in 2026** so far: Paramount+ (January), Prime Video's ad-free tier (April), Netflix (spring), Peacock and Apple TV+ (both August), and ESPN plus Disney+/Hulu (both September).
- **The two most recent hikes just landed.** ESPN's Unlimited and Select tiers went up September 17, and Disney+ and Hulu followed on September 23 — both now push their ad-free plans above $21/month.
- **Stacking eight major ad-free services now runs about $139/month** (~$1,670/year), per a September 2026 tally — within a few dollars of what an inflation-adjusted cable bill from a decade ago would cost.
- **This isn't a one-off.** Several of these services have now raised prices in each of the last three or four years running — treat an annual hike as the new normal, not a surprise.

Streaming was supposed to be the cheap alternative to cable. In 2026, "streamflation" — the industry's own shorthand for this — has closed most of that gap. Here's every price change we can confirm this year, in order, with sources.

## The 2026 timeline

| Date | Service | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 15 | Paramount+ | Essential (ads) $7.99 → $8.99/mo; Premium (ad-free) $12.99 → $13.99/mo |

| Aug 28 | Apple TV+ | $12.99 → $14.99/mo; annual plan $99 → $119/yr |

A few things stand out. First, the pace: seven confirmed increases across nine months, roughly one a month once you hit summer. Second, the ad-supported tiers aren't exempt anymore — Peacock, Paramount+, and Disney+/Hulu all nudged their ad tiers up too, just by smaller amounts than their ad-free plans. Third, Disney's two hikes three weeks apart (ESPN, then Disney+/Hulu) landed back-to-back on the same households, since ESPN is now sold bundled with Disney+ and Hulu.

## What a full stack costs now

Adding up eight major ad-free services — Netflix, Disney+, Hulu, Max, Paramount+, Peacock, Apple TV+, and Prime Video's ad-free tier — at September 2026 list prices comes to roughly **$139/month, or about $1,670/year**, before any bundle discount. Swapping in ad-supported tiers wherever they're offered brings that down closer to $90/month. Either way, that's a meaningfully bigger number than it was even a year ago, purely from list-price increases on services most households already had — not from adding anything new.

## What's NOT worth doing about it

- **Panic-cancelling everything.** A price hike of $1-3/month on a service you actually watch weekly rarely changes the math versus cable. Cancel what you've stopped watching, not what just got $2 pricier.
- **Switching to a "cheaper" live-TV bundle as the fix.** Live-TV streaming (YouTube TV, Fubo, Sling) was already priced close to cable before this year's hikes — see [is cutting the cord still worth it](/guides/is-cutting-the-cord-worth-it-2026) for that math. It's not where the savings are.
- **Assuming every bundle is now a bad deal.** ESPN and Disney+/Hulu both went up, but the official three-app bundle can still undercut buying them separately now that all three cost more alone too — the gap just moved. Check current bundle pricing against separate pricing before assuming either way; see [which streaming bundles actually save money](/guides/streaming-bundles-worth-it-2026).

## What actually helps

1. **Move to ad-supported tiers where you can tolerate it.** Every service in this year's hikes still raised its ad tier by less than its ad-free tier — the gap between them keeps widening in the ad tier's favor.
2. **Re-run the numbers on official bundles.** When two or three services you use each go up individually, a bundle that hasn't widened its own price gap can go from "meh" to "worth it."
3. **Drop the service, not just complain about the price.** If a hike is the push you needed to finally cancel something you weren't watching, that's the whole point of no-contract streaming — use it.
4. **Recheck annually, not just when a hike hits.** With increases landing roughly monthly across the industry in 2026, your stack's total cost six months from now probably isn't the same as it is today.
