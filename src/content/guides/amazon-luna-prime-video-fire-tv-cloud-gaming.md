## The short version

- **What happened:** Amazon added a **Games tab** to the Prime Video app, powered by its Luna cloud-gaming service — playable at no extra cost for Prime members on Fire TV, no download or install required.
- **Where:** US and UK only, on Fire TV devices, as of the week of July 23, 2026.
- **What you get:** a rotating free library mixing party games (Clue, Ticket to Ride, Taboo — played with your phone as the controller) and bigger titles like Hogwarts Legacy, EA Sports FC 26, and Indiana Jones and the Great Circle (which need a real Bluetooth controller).
- **The catch:** it's US/UK-and-Fire-TV-only for now, the free catalog is smaller than paid Luna+, and Amazon hasn't said exactly which Fire TV models qualify.
- **The bigger picture:** this is Amazon's latest push to make cloud gaming an ambient feature of a streamer you already own, following NVIDIA's GeForce Now on Shield and Microsoft's own cloud-streaming experiments.

If your Fire TV's Prime Video app suddenly has a "Games" icon next to Movies and TV, this is why — and whether it's worth your attention depends a lot on which Fire TV you own and what you actually want to play.

## What Amazon actually shipped

Amazon has owned Luna, its cloud-gaming service, since 2020, but it's always lived in its own separate app — one more thing to download, sign into, and remember exists. The change this week folds a slice of that service directly into **Prime Video**, the app that's already open on your TV far more often than any dedicated gaming app.

Open Prime Video on a supported Fire TV now and you'll see a new **Games** tab sitting next to Movies, TV, Live TV, Sports, and News. Tap into it and you get instant access to a free library — no Luna+ subscription, no separate purchase, just whatever Prime membership you already pay for.

The catalog splits into two flavors:

- **GameNight party titles** — Clue, Ticket to Ride, Taboo, and similar couch-multiplayer games designed around the free **Luna Controller** phone app, so nobody needs an actual gamepad to join in.
- **Bigger single-player and sports titles** — Hogwarts Legacy, EA Sports FC 26, Indiana Jones and the Great Circle, and Masters of the Universe: Legends Unite, which do need a paired Bluetooth controller for a normal console-style experience.

Amazon says it will keep adding new titles to the free rotation every month, though it hasn't committed to a fixed library size.

## Which Fire TV devices actually get it

This is the least-documented part of the rollout. Amazon's language is "compatible Fire TV devices" in the US and UK — not a model list. That's not unusual for cloud gaming: because the actual game runs on Amazon's servers and your device is just decoding a video stream and sending back button presses, the local hardware bar is much lower than for a game that has to render itself.

In practice, that means it's plausible the feature reaches most current Fire TV hardware, including devices already in our reviews: the [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-max-2024) and the [Fire TV Cube](/reviews/fire-tv-cube-3rd-gen) — our review of the Cube already noted that Luna titles "run smoothly" on its octa-core chip, well before this Prime Video integration existed. Amazon's newest budget stick, the [Fire TV Stick 4K Select](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-select-vega-os), runs the newer Linux-based Vega OS with a more locked-down app ecosystem than older Fire TV Sticks; we haven't seen Amazon confirm whether the Games tab reaches Vega OS devices yet, so if you specifically bought that stick for this feature, check for the Games tab before assuming it's there.

If you don't see a Games tab yet, it's most likely a staged rollout — the same pattern Amazon used for [this year's Fire TV interface redesign](/guides/new-fire-tv-interface-2026), which also reached devices in waves rather than all at once.

## How this compares to GeForce Now and Xbox Cloud Gaming

Amazon isn't the only company betting that your streaming box can double as a game console:

- **NVIDIA GeForce Now**, available on the [Shield TV Pro](/reviews/nvidia-shield-tv-pro-2025), is the most mature option here — a paid subscription that streams your own PC game library (Steam, Epic, and more) at higher performance tiers, rather than a curated free catalog.
- **Xbox Cloud Gaming** (bundled with Game Pass Ultimate) is Microsoft's answer, and Microsoft has reportedly been testing an ad-supported, lower-cost streaming tier of its own around the same time as this Luna news — a sign the whole industry is chasing the same "no console required" pitch.
- **Amazon Luna in Prime Video** sits a notch below both on raw game selection and performance ambition, but it wins on friction: there's nothing to subscribe to or download if you already pay for Prime, which is the point. It's a discovery funnel for casual players, not a replacement for a dedicated cloud-gaming subscription if you're already invested in one.

If you specifically want the deepest cloud-gaming library on a streaming box today, GeForce Now on the Shield remains the stronger dedicated pick. If you just want to see what cloud gaming feels like without spending anything extra, the new Prime Video tab is the lower-effort on-ramp.

## What's NOT worth getting excited about (yet)

- **It's US/UK-and-Fire-TV-only.** No word yet on other countries, other platforms (Roku, Google TV, Apple TV), or a timeline for either.
- **The free catalog is thinner than Luna+.** The $9.99/month Luna+ tier in the standalone Luna app still has a much bigger library — this integration is a taste, not a full replacement.
- **No confirmed device list.** "Compatible Fire TV devices" is vague enough that budget or older sticks may or may not get it, and Amazon hasn't clarified.
- **You'll still want a real controller for the good stuff.** The phone-as-controller party games are fine for a group hangout, but titles like Hogwarts Legacy and EA Sports FC 26 are a much better experience with an actual Bluetooth gamepad — budget for one if this is what tips you toward trying it seriously.

None of that makes the feature bad — it's a genuinely clever use of an app you already have open — just don't expect it to replace a real cloud-gaming subscription or console.
