## The short version

- DIRECTV rolled out **Build Your Own Multiview** on **October 13, 2026** — a custom grid of up to **four live sports channels** on one screen, instead of a fixed layout.
- It's **not available everywhere yet**. Right now it only works on DIRECTV's own **Gemini puck** and on **Roku** devices. Android TV, Fire TV, Apple TV, Vizio, LG, and Tizen TVs are "coming," with no date attached.
- It requires a **Signature package or Genre Pack** subscription — DIRECTV's base plans don't get it.
- Eligible channels are mostly sports networks: **ESPN, FS1, NFL Network, NBA TV, the Big Ten Network, and the SEC Network**, plus local/other channels only while they're airing a qualifying live game.
- Reviewers calling it a real improvement also note it's still a step behind the multiview YouTube TV and Fubo already offer — DIRECTV is catching up, not leading.

If you're a DIRECTV subscriber who's been stuck toggling between single-channel views during a busy Sunday, here's exactly what changed, what it needs, and whether it's worth caring about if you're not already a DIRECTV customer.

## What actually launched

DIRECTV calls it "Build Your Own Multiview," and the name is the feature: instead of a pre-set split screen, you pick the channels yourself. Start watching a game, then add up to three more sports channels to the same screen, arranging them in a two-, three-, or four-way grid. The channel you started on stays as the anchor; you add and drop the others on the fly rather than committing to a fixed layout up front.

It's aimed squarely at the Sunday-afternoon problem: football kicks off across four or five games at once, your Red Zone-style whip-around app isn't showing the specific matchup you care about, and the old DIRECTV multiview only let you combine channels DIRECTV had pre-paired. Build Your Own Multiview removes that restriction for a defined list of sports networks — ESPN, FS1, NFL Network, NBA TV, the Big Ten Network, and the SEC Network — plus local or other national channels, but only while they're actually carrying an eligible live sporting event, not as a general-purpose pick.

It launched to subscribers on DIRECTV's **Signature package or a Genre Pack**. If you're on a smaller base tier, this feature isn't part of what you're paying for — DIRECTV hasn't said whether that will change.

## Which devices actually have it today

This is the part that matters most if you're deciding whether to care right now: **Build Your Own Multiview is not a universal DIRECTV feature yet.** It shipped first on two platforms:

- **DIRECTV Gemini** — DIRECTV's own 4K streaming puck, the hardware the company sells directly for its internet-delivered service.
- **Roku devices** — including the [Roku Ultra](/reviews/roku-ultra-2024) and [Roku Streaming Stick 4K](/reviews/roku-streaming-stick-4k-2023), running the DIRECTV app.

DIRECTV has publicly said **Android TV, Fire TV, Apple TV, Vizio, LG, and Samsung Tizen TVs** are next, but as of this writing there's no confirmed date for any of them. If you watch DIRECTV on a Fire TV Stick or Apple TV today, the feature simply isn't there — don't assume an app update is about to drop it on your device, since DIRECTV hasn't committed to a timeline.

Practically, that means **Roku is currently the only general-purpose streaming-device platform where this works.** If you're shopping for hardware specifically to get the most out of a DIRECTV subscription for sports, a Roku box is the safer near-term bet over a Fire TV or Apple TV until DIRECTV confirms the other rollouts — see our [best streaming device for live sports guide](/guides/best-streaming-device-for-live-sports-2026) for the wider picture on hardware that holds up during a packed Sunday, Ethernet port and all.

## What's NOT worth getting excited about yet

Candor first: this is a genuine improvement for DIRECTV subscribers, but it's not a reason to switch services, and it's not as capable as what competitors already ship.

- **It's behind YouTube TV and Fubo's multiview, not ahead of it.** Both of those live-TV services already offer more flexible multi-game grids, and reviewers have pointed out DIRECTV is closing a gap rather than setting a new standard. If multiview sports viewing is your main priority and you're not already locked into DIRECTV, those two remain the more mature options.
- **It doesn't help if you're not eligible.** Base-tier DIRECTV subscribers don't get it at all, and the channel list is specifically limited to sports networks carrying live events — you can't use it as a general four-channel news or entertainment grid.
- **It's not a reason to buy new hardware today**, unless you're specifically buying for DIRECTV on Roku. If you're an Apple TV or Fire TV household, there's nothing to switch devices for yet — you'd just be waiting on an unannounced app update either way.

## Who should actually care

If you're already a DIRECTV subscriber on a Signature or Genre Pack plan, watching on a Gemini puck or a Roku device, this is a straightforward win for Sunday afternoons and should show up automatically in your DIRECTV app — no setup beyond picking your channels in the grid. If you're shopping for a live-TV service purely to compare multiview options, it's worth knowing DIRECTV has entered the conversation, but it's not yet the reason to pick DIRECTV over an established multiview service. And if your football-watching setup already revolves around an [antenna plus streaming apps](/guides/how-to-watch-nfl-2026-without-cable) rather than a traditional satellite/live-TV package, this change doesn't affect you at all.

**Which devices support it right now?** Only DIRECTV's Gemini puck and Roku devices. Android TV, Fire TV, Apple TV, Vizio, LG, and Tizen TV support is planned but undated.

**Which channels work in it?** ESPN, FS1, NFL Network, NBA TV, the Big Ten Network, and the SEC Network, plus other channels only while airing an eligible live game.

**Do I need a specific DIRECTV plan?** Yes — it's limited to the Signature package or a Genre Pack; DIRECTV's base plans don't include it.
