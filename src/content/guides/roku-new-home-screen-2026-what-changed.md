## The short version

- Roku shipped its **first major home-screen redesign in more than a decade**, rolling out gradually from **May 27, 2026**.
- The headline changes: a new **Quick Access** row of frequently-used apps, a **"Top Picks for You"** section that grew from 3 tiles to 5 and now mixes personal recommendations with paid placements, and a taller **ad marquee** along the right edge.
- There's **no built-in "revert to classic" switch** — but Settings lets you hide the new rows and set tiles back to the old 3-per-row size, which gets you most of the way to the calmer layout.
- It applies to **every Roku device**, not just new ones — your existing [Roku Streaming Stick 4K](/reviews/roku-streaming-stick-4k-2023) or [Roku Ultra](/reviews/roku-ultra-2024) got it as a software update, no purchase required.

If your Roku suddenly looks different, you're not imagining it, and it's not a bug. Here's exactly what changed and how to dial it back.

## What actually changed

Roku's old home screen — a plain grid of app tiles, largely unchanged since the mid-2010s — is being replaced across the fleet. The rollout is gradual (not everyone got it the same week), but by mid-2026 most active Roku TVs and players in the U.S. are on the new layout. Three changes matter most:

**Quick Access panel.** A new row sits at the top of the screen surfacing the 8–12 apps you actually open most. You can pin a specific app to it by highlighting the tile, pressing the `*` button, and choosing "Add to Quick Access" — or hide the row entirely if you'd rather keep the classic all-apps grid front and center.

**"Top Picks for You" got bigger — and more commercial.** This used to be a small row of three suggested titles. It's now a five-tile row with larger artwork, and it blends genuine personal recommendations with sponsored placements — shows, movies, and apps that advertisers paid to appear in what looks like a personalized list.

**A new ad marquee.** A vertical strip of promoted content now runs down the right side of the home screen alongside the row layout, which is new; the old grid didn't reserve dedicated ad real estate outside the single corner banner.

Roku has described the update as being about personalization — using the same viewing-habit signals that power content recommendations to also power ad targeting, so the promoted content feels closer to what you'd actually click. Whether that's a good trade depends on how much you mind seeing more anticipated content in place of a plain app grid.

## Why Roku did this

Roku's core business isn't hardware — the players are sold near cost. The money is in advertising and platform fees, and the home screen is Roku's single most valuable ad slot: it's the first thing tens of millions of TVs show, every single day. A redesign that increases both engagement (better recommendations mean people find something to watch faster) and ad inventory (bigger, more prominent promoted tiles) is a very rational move for that business model — even if it makes the interface feel busier for anyone who just wants to open Netflix.

It's worth saying plainly: this is not a bug or an overzealous default you can fully switch off. The ad-supported home screen is the product now, on every Roku device, old or new.

## How to make it feel calmer

You can't get the old layout back exactly, but a few settings changes remove most of the clutter:

- **Hide Quick Access:** Settings → Home Screen → Quick Access → Hide. The App Grid takes its place instead.
- **Hide Recommendation Rows:** Settings → Home Screen → Recommendation Rows → Hide. This removes the expanded "Top Picks for You" section.
- **Set Tile Size to Large:** Settings → Home Screen → Tile Size → Large. This restores the old-style 3-tiles-per-row grid instead of the denser new default.
- **Limit ad tracking:** Settings → Privacy → Advertising → limit ad tracking. This won't remove ads, but it stops them from being built on your specific viewing history.

Combined, these four changes get you close to the old experience: a clean grid of your apps, no recommendation carousel, no Quick Access row. What you can't undo is the ad marquee — it's part of the new layout's structure, not an optional row.

## What's not worth doing

- **Don't expect a firmware downgrade to fix it.** The redesign is server-side and tied to your Roku account, not just the software version on the box — rolling back firmware doesn't restore the old home screen.
- **Don't bother hunting for a "classic mode" hidden setting.** Cord-cutting forums have circulated a few of these; as of this redesign, Roku has not shipped one. The Settings changes above are the real options.
- **Don't buy a new Roku device expecting a cleaner interface.** Every current Roku product ships with this same home screen — a new [Roku Ultra](/reviews/roku-ultra-2024) or [Streaming Stick 4K](/reviews/roku-streaming-stick-4k-2023) looks identical to an existing one that's taken the update.

Not really.
