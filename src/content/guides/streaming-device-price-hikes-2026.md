## Why streaming devices suddenly cost more

Streaming sticks and boxes are small computers: a chip, a bit of RAM, and flash storage in a plastic shell. Through 2026, the global supply of memory chips got tight as AI data centers — which need enormous amounts of RAM and NAND flash for training and inference hardware — started buying up a large share of available manufacturing capacity. Reporting on the shortage (some outlets have nicknamed it "RAMageddon") describes memory-chip makers redirecting production toward higher-margin AI customers, squeezing supply for everyday consumer electronics.

Streaming devices are cheap partly *because* they use small amounts of commodity memory — a $49 stick might have only 2–4GB of RAM and 8–16GB of storage. That makes them unusually sensitive to a memory-price spike: a $5–10 jump in component cost is a much bigger percentage of a $49 product than it is of a $1,000 laptop. Manufacturers passed that cost increase straight to the shelf price rather than absorb it.

This is a genuinely different story from the usual streaming-cost conversation. It's not a subscription price hike or a "streamflation" story about Netflix and Disney+ — it's the one-time hardware purchase, the thing that was supposed to be the *cheap* part of cutting the cord, getting more expensive too.

## What's NOT worth doing right now

- **Panic-buying at full list price.** If a device you want just jumped from $49 to $80, that's exactly the moment to wait for a sale rather than lock in the peak price.
- **Assuming "on sale" always means a real discount.** Some retailers are advertising the *new* higher price as the "regular" price and discounting from there — check a device's price history (or our own reviews, which note original launch pricing) before trusting a sale badge.
- **Overpaying for a barely-newer model.** If last year's device is functionally identical and available for less, the memory shortage is a bad reason to chase the newest SKU. See our [newest streaming tech roundup](/guides/newest-streaming-devices-tech-2026) for what's actually new versus just renamed.
- **Buying secondhand from an unverified seller to "beat" the hike.** Used streaming sticks are a common scam vector precisely because demand is up — stick to retailers with real return policies.

## FAQ

**Why did streaming device prices go up in 2026?** A memory-chip (RAM/NAND) shortage tied to AI data-center demand raised the cost of the components inside every streaming stick and box, and makers passed that cost on.

**Which devices got hit hardest?** Reports point to Roku's lineup and budget Onn sticks seeing some of the steepest percentage increases, with Google TV Streamer and Apple TV 4K also reported moving up significantly.

**Is this the same as streaming *service* price hikes?** No — this is about the one-time hardware purchase. Subscription prices are a separate (and ongoing) story; see our [cord-cutting cost breakdown](/guides/real-cost-of-cutting-the-cord-2026) for that side of it.
