## The short version

- **What happened:** Roku, Amazon, Apple, and Walmart have all raised prices on streaming hardware in 2026 — in some cases by 40% or more on a single device.
- **Why:** a global shortage of conventional DRAM and NAND memory chips, as manufacturers redirect factory capacity toward the far more profitable high-bandwidth memory that powers AI data centers.
- **How bad:** industry reporting puts conventional DRAM contract prices up roughly double from where they started the year, with data centers now consuming the majority of global memory output.
- **What to do:** don't wait it out. Analysts don't expect meaningful relief until new memory fabs ramp up around 2027-2028, so a device that costs more today is unlikely to get cheaper next quarter.

If your usual $30-$50 streaming stick suddenly rang up at $50-$85, you didn't imagine it. Here's what's actually driving it, which devices got hit hardest, and what's still a sane buy right now.

## The price hikes, by brand

**Amazon Fire TV.** Amazon raised prices across its entire Fire TV lineup, and the increases scale up with the device: the entry-level Fire TV Stick HD moved from $34.99 to $39.99, the Fire TV Stick 4K Select from $39.99 to $49.99, the [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-max-2024) from $59.99 to $84.99, and the [Fire TV Cube](/reviews/fire-tv-cube-3rd-gen) from $139.99 to $199.99 — a roughly 43% jump on Amazon's flagship box.

**Roku.** Roku's lineup-wide increase pushed the base Streaming Stick from $29.99 to $39.99, the [Streaming Stick 4K](/reviews/roku-streaming-stick-4k-2023) from $49.99 to $79.99, and the [Roku Ultra](/reviews/roku-ultra-2024) from $99.99 to $149.99 — its steepest hikes yet on a platform that built its reputation on being the budget-friendly option.

**Apple.** The [Apple TV 4K](/reviews/apple-tv-4k-3rd-gen) jumped from its long-standing $129 to $199, with the higher-storage Ethernet model climbing to $249. Apple didn't stop at hardware, either: on August 28, it raised Apple TV+ subscriptions from $12.99 to $14.99 a month (annual plans from $99 to $119), and bumped the Apple One bundle from $19.95 to $21.95 a month.

**Walmart's Onn.** The budget king took the biggest relative hit. The entry Onn streaming stick has roughly doubled in price over the past year through two separate increases, and the newest [Onn 4K Pro](/reviews/onn-4k-pro-streaming-box) refresh launched around $10 higher than the model it replaces — even as it's been selling out fast enough that resellers are flipping it near double retail.

**The relative holdout.** Not everyone raised prices by the same margin — the [Google TV Streamer](/reviews/google-tv-streamer-4k-2024) has stayed closer to its original $99 price point than most competitors, making it one of the better-value picks left in the current market.

## Why: an AI-driven memory chip shortage

This isn't a streaming-industry problem — it's a semiconductor one. Every streaming stick and box relies on ordinary DRAM and NAND flash memory, the same commodity chips used in phones, laptops, and routers. Demand for that memory hasn't dropped; what's changed is who chipmakers sell it to first.

AI data centers need enormous volumes of high-bandwidth memory to train and run large models, and they pay far more per chip for it than consumer electronics makers ever have. Manufacturers have responded by shifting factory capacity toward that higher-margin AI memory, which leaves less conventional DRAM and NAND for everything else — and pushes what's left to a higher price. Multiple industry trackers report conventional DRAM contract prices roughly doubling since the start of 2026, with data centers now absorbing the majority of global memory chip output.

That's the mechanism behind every price tag above: a $50 streaming stick isn't $50 anymore not because Roku or Amazon decided to charge more for the same box, but because the memory chip inside it costs meaningfully more to buy than it did a year ago.

## What's NOT worth doing right now

- **Don't wait for a sale that reverses this.** Doorbuster pricing on last year's stock will still show up around the holidays, but the structural shortage behind these hikes isn't a temporary blip — most estimates put real relief out to 2027 or 2028, once new memory fab capacity comes online. Waiting on the theory that prices will drop back to 2025 levels is a bad bet.
- **Don't overpay for a flagship you don't need.** The steepest percentage increases hit the higher-end boxes — the Fire TV Cube and Roku Ultra both jumped over 40-50%. If you were on the fence between a mid-range stick and the flagship, the price gap between them just got a lot less forgiving, so it's worth double-checking whether you actually need the extra horsepower.
- **Don't assume a review's listed price is current.** Prices are moving fast enough in 2026 that a review written even a few months ago may quote a pre-hike number. Always check a device's review page for its live price before you buy — see our [cheap streaming device roundup](/guides/best-cheap-streaming-device-under-50-2026) for how the sub-$50 tier looks now that the bar has moved.

## What to actually buy

If you need a device now, don't overthink it: pick based on what you use it for, not on trying to time a market that isn't coming back down soon. The [Google TV Streamer](/reviews/google-tv-streamer-4k-2024) is the strongest value pick at the moment since it's held its price better than most rivals. If budget is the priority above all else, a basic Android TV box like the [Xiaomi Mi Box S](/reviews/xiaomi-mi-box-s-4k) is still comparatively affordable, though you should confirm current pricing before buying rather than assuming last year's tag holds.
