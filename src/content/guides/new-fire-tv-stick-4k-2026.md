## The short version

- **What launched:** a redesigned $59.99 Fire TV Stick 4K, shipping since September 30, 2026, that **replaces both** the Fire TV Stick 4K Select and the Fire TV Stick 4K Plus with one model.
- **Still Vega OS:** like the [Select it replaces](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-select-vega-os), it runs Amazon's Linux-based Vega OS — no APK sideloading, and it's the first "Fire TV Stick 4K" ever without Dolby Vision.
- **What's actually new:** a redesigned, more tactile remote you can navigate by feel, a "Direct Power" option that can draw power from your TV's own USB port, and an Alexa+ hub built into the Fire TV interface.
- **Who it's for:** people who stick to Netflix, Prime Video, and Disney+ and want cheap, fast 4K. If you want Dolby Vision or sideloading, the [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-max-2024) is still the one to buy.

Amazon just simplified its Fire TV Stick lineup and, in the process, locked in Vega OS as the default experience for anyone buying a Fire TV Stick that isn't the top-tier Max. Here's exactly what changed, what it's like on paper, and whether it's actually worth your $60.

## What Amazon actually changed

The headline move is consolidation. Amazon had been selling two separate budget-to-mid 4K sticks — the $40 Fire TV Stick 4K Select and a Fire TV Stick 4K Plus — and replaced both with a single new Fire TV Stick 4K at $59.99. It started shipping September 30, 2026, and briefly dropped to $29.99 in the US during Prime Big Deal Days on October 6–7 — treat that as a limited-time promo, not the real price going forward.

That puts the new stick squarely between the $39.99 Fire TV Stick HD and the Android-based Fire TV Stick 4K Max, which has gotten noticeably pricier itself this year as part of the broader [2026 streaming-hardware price hikes](/guides/streaming-device-price-hikes-2026) driven by the memory-chip shortage. In other words: Amazon is using the cheaper-to-build Vega OS platform to hold a sub-$60 4K option in the lineup while its Android-based hardware keeps climbing.

Under the hood, the new stick runs a quad-core 1.7GHz chip with 1GB of RAM and 8GB of storage, over Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.3. That's a modest spec sheet, but it matches what the outgoing Select already did reasonably well for mainstream streaming — opening Netflix, Prime Video, and Disney+ quickly isn't a heavy lift for any current-generation stick.

## The new remote

The actual physical upgrade here is the remote, not the stick. Amazon redesigned the standard Fire TV Remote with an asymmetric shape and more pronounced buttons, so you can find play/pause, the directional pad, and the customizable shortcut button by feel instead of looking down at it — a feature that used to be exclusive to the pricier Alexa Voice Remote Pro.

The new remote ships with the stick, and Amazon is also selling it separately for $14.99, with standalone availability starting in late October 2026. A step-up **Fire TV Remote Plus** — aluminum housing, backlit buttons, and an Alexa-powered "Find My Remote" feature — is coming later, but not until early 2027, and it isn't bundled with this stick.

Amazon also added a "Direct Power" option: the stick can draw power straight from a USB port on your TV using the included cable, instead of needing a separate wall outlet. If your TV has a spare USB port, that's one less cable to hide behind the set or pack for travel.

## The catch: it's still Vega OS

This is the part that matters if you've ever sideloaded an app on a Fire Stick. Vega OS is Amazon's own Linux-based platform — not Android — and it carries the same restrictions we already flagged when the Select launched:

- **No sideloading, at all.** Vega OS blocks APK installation at the system level. Kodi, third-party launchers, and the IPTV/emulator apps some cord-cutters rely on simply aren't installable. See our [deeper look at Amazon's sideloading crackdown](/guides/fire-tv-sideloading-blocked-2026) for how this fits the bigger picture across Fire TV.
- **No Dolby Vision.** The new stick supports HDR10+ and HLG only — making it the first device to ever carry the "Fire TV Stick 4K" name without Dolby Vision since the line launched in 2018.
- **A smaller, newer app store.** Amazon says more than 2,000 apps have been ported to Vega OS since its first device launched, including Spotify, Xbox, and VLC — real progress, but still thinner than the long-established Fire OS catalog.

None of that is a change from the Select — it's confirmation that Vega OS, not Fire OS, is now Amazon's default for every Fire TV Stick except the Max. If you specifically want an Android-based, sideload-friendly Fire TV, the [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-max-2024) or [Fire TV Cube](/reviews/fire-tv-cube-3rd-gen) are the only options left.

## What's NOT worth getting excited about

- **The Alexa+ hub.** Amazon is pairing this launch with an Alexa+ hub built into the Fire TV interface, but it's the same conversational-search story we've already covered in our [AI-on-TV-boxes breakdown](/guides/ai-on-tv-box-2026) — useful, not revolutionary, and not unique to this device.
- **"Direct Power" as a headline feature.** Drawing power from your TV's USB port is a nice convenience, not a technical leap — plenty of sticks have supported USB power from a TV for years.
- **The $29.99 Prime Day price.** That was a short promotional window, not the device's real price. Budget for $59.99, and check [our deals page](/deals) before assuming a sale price is still live.
- **Waiting for the Remote Plus.** If you want backlit buttons and aluminum styling, that remote isn't shipping until early 2027 — don't delay a purchase for it.

## Should you buy it?

Buy the new Fire TV Stick 4K if you only use the big streaming apps, don't care about sideloading, and want a fast, cheap 4K stick with a genuinely nicer remote than before. At $59.99 it's a reasonable, if unexciting, upgrade over the Select it replaces.

Skip it if you want Dolby Vision, Wi-Fi 6E, or the ability to sideload Kodi or other apps — for any of that, spend more on the Android-based [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](/reviews/fire-tv-stick-4k-max-2024), which remains the only non-Cube Fire TV Stick that still runs Fire OS. And if the operating system itself bothers you, a [Google TV Streamer](/reviews/google-tv-streamer-4k-2024) sidesteps the Vega-vs-Fire-OS question entirely.
