## The short version

- **What changed:** Discovery+'s ad-supported plan went from $5.99 to $6.99/month, and its ad-free plan went from $9.99 to $11.99/month — the service's second US price increase since January 2025.
- **Who's feeling it now:** new subscribers paid the higher price immediately when it was announced in mid-October 2026, but most existing subscribers are only seeing it hit their bill around November 12, 2026 or later, after a required 30-day notice.
- **Why now:** the hike landed about a week after Skydance completed its takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery — a pattern familiar from every other big "new owner, new price" moment in streaming this year.
- **The verdict:** still a reasonable deal if you actually watch HGTV, Food Network, TLC, or Discovery's true-crime lineup regularly. Weak value if you log in once a month "just in case."

Discovery+ has quietly become one of the cheaper line items on a lot of streaming bills — which is exactly why a $1-to-$2 bump is easy to miss until it's already happened. Here's what actually changed, and whether it still earns its spot in your subscription stack.

## What actually changed

Discovery+'s new US pricing, confirmed by multiple outlets covering the change:

| Plan | Old price | New price |
|---|---|---|
| Ad-supported | $5.99/mo | $6.99/mo |
| Ad-free | $9.99/mo | $11.99/mo |

The increase was announced in mid-October 2026, roughly a week after Skydance's acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery closed — making this the first pricing move under the service's new ownership. New subscribers were charged the higher rate right away. Existing subscribers were given the standard 30 days' notice, with the new amount appearing on billing statements on or after November 12, 2026 — which means plenty of subscribers are only now opening their bank app and wondering why Discovery+ got more expensive.

It's worth keeping the increase in proportion: this is the service's **second** US hike since January 2025, when the ad-supported tier moved from $4.99 to $5.99 and the ad-free tier from $8.99 to $9.99. Two dollar-and-change increases in under two years is a real trend, but Discovery+ is still priced well below the major on-demand services — [HBO Max's](/guides/every-streaming-price-hike-2026-tracked) ad-free plan alone runs well north of $18/month, for comparison.

One wrinkle: coverage of the change notes that the increase applies to subscribers who pay for Discovery+ directly, not to those who get it bundled through another plan. If you're not sure which camp you're in, check your most recent billing statement or the subscription screen inside the app rather than guessing.

Zoom out and this is the same pattern that's played out across the industry all year: a service changes hands or restructures, and a price adjustment follows within weeks. Discovery+ is a smaller, cheaper line item than the Netflix, Disney+, or ESPN hikes that made bigger headlines in 2026, which is exactly why it's easy to miss — a $1 or $2 bump on an $6-to-$12 service doesn't generate the same outrage as a flagship service crossing $20, even though the percentage increase (roughly 17-20%) is actually steeper.

## What you're actually paying for

Discovery+ is a single-genre streamer, and that's both its strength and its limit. The library leans almost entirely on unscripted television: **HGTV** (home renovation and real estate), **Food Network**, **TLC**, **Discovery Channel**, **Investigation Discovery** (true crime), **Animal Planet**, **Travel Channel**, and **Magnolia Network**. If that's a genre you watch a lot of — and plenty of households quietly do — it's a deep, constantly refreshed library that no other single service matches.

What it *doesn't* have is prestige scripted drama, blockbuster films, or the kind of must-watch original series that drives water-cooler conversation. That content lives over on Max, Discovery+'s much pricier sibling under the same corporate roof. Discovery+ is a complement to a "real" streaming stack, not a replacement for one — see [every streaming price hike in 2026, tracked](/guides/every-streaming-price-hike-2026-tracked) for how it fits alongside everything else you're probably already paying for, and the [streaming cost calculator](/cost-calculator) to see what your specific mix actually adds up to.

The library also refreshes fast. New seasons of HGTV renovation shows, Food Network competition formats, and TLC's long-running reality franchises typically land on Discovery+ close to their original broadcast window, so it functions less like an archive and more like a second, cheaper cable package for a specific slice of programming. That's a meaningfully different value proposition than a general-purpose streamer, and it's worth judging it on those terms rather than comparing it dollar-for-dollar against a service built around prestige drama.

## Who should keep it, who should cut it

**Keep it if:** you genuinely watch HGTV renovation shows, Food Network competitions, or ID true-crime marathons on a regular basis. At $6.99 with ads, Discovery+ is still one of the cheapest subscriptions most households carry — cheaper than a single fast-food combo meal — and the price increase doesn't change that math much.

**Cut it if:** you signed up for one show, finished it months ago, and haven't opened the app since. This is exactly the kind of subscription that survives on inertia, and a $1-to-$2 increase is a good trigger to actually check your watch history before renewing again. Free, ad-supported apps carry a meaningful amount of overlapping reality and lifestyle content — it's not a perfect substitute for Discovery+'s current-season library, but it's close enough for casual viewers to not miss much.

**If you're on the fence:** the ad-supported plan at $6.99 is the better test than cancelling outright. It's cheap enough to keep around for a season while you decide, without committing to the ad-free price. ## FAQ

- **How much did it go up?** $1 on the ad-supported plan (to $6.99), $2 on the ad-free plan (to $11.99).
- **When does it hit my bill?** On or after November 12, 2026, for existing subscribers, following 30 days' notice.
- **Does my bundle protect me?** Possibly — the hike is reported to apply to direct subscribers, not bundled ones. Check your statement to be sure.
- **Worth keeping?** Yes, if you're a regular viewer of its unscripted lineup. Skip it if you're only an occasional watcher — the gap between its genre and free ad-supported alternatives is smaller than the price difference.
