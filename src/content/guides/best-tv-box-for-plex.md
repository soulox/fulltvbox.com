## Why Your TV Box Choice Matters for Plex

Plex is not like Netflix. While Netflix sends a pre-encoded stream to your device, Plex may need to transcode video on the fly if your device can't direct play the file format. The more powerful your device, the more likely it can handle direct play — skipping transcoding entirely and delivering the best possible quality.

## What to Look for in a Plex Device

**Direct play support:** Your device needs to support the video codec (H.264, H.265/HEVC), audio codec (AAC, AC3, DTS, Dolby TrueHD), and container (MKV, MP4, AVI) of your files. If it doesn't, Plex transcodes — which requires server CPU power.

**Ethernet:** Wired connections are essential for 4K direct play. Wi-Fi works for 1080p but can stutter on large 4K files.

**RAM:** 2GB is the minimum. 4GB+ means the app stays loaded in memory between sessions.

**Plex Pass:** Unlocks offline sync, live TV, hardware-accelerated transcoding, and mobile sync. Worth it if you use Plex heavily.

## Devices to Avoid for Plex

- **Roku** — The Plex app exists but is noticeably worse than on Android TV. Limited codec support means more transcoding.
- **Apple TV** — Good hardware but the Plex app is less capable than Android TV. No server hosting.
- **Budget Android boxes under $30** — Slow processors force transcoding on everything.
